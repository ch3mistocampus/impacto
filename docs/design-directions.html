Impacto: UI review and five design directions

A review of the current Next.js UI (public site, donor deck, field intake, coordinator desk), followed by five distinct visual directions. Every mock uses the same content so you can compare them directly: one need for 200 bricks with 80 pledged, 50 received and delivered, 40 used and 30 verified.

Review of the current UI

Based on the source in src/app, src/components/* and globals.css. I didn't capture runtime screenshots because the app needs a live Convex deployment.

Verdict: The product thinking is strong. Keeping the six ledger stages honest, building in privacy and giving every swipe a button alternative are better than most hackathon UIs. Four things weaken the visual layer: missing image assets mean emoji stand in for imagery, each color carries two or three meanings, three different component styles are in use, and warnings repeat until people stop reading them. Some status chips and the dark mode also fail contrast checks.

What's working

  • The ledger never stacks. LedgerBar draws each of the six stages as its own course, pledges are hatched and only verified items are green. This is the best idea in the codebase. Every direction below keeps it.
  • Accessibility is built in. Swipes have large-button equivalents with aria-keyshortcuts. The UI also handles reduced motion, uses :focus-visible, announces ledger updates with aria-live and has proper tab roles on the coordinator desk.
  • The copy has a voice. “Cada ladrillo, contado desde la necesidad hasta el techo terminado” and “Sin pines en el mapa. Sin nombres.” are good lines.
  • Field intake is resilient. It offers a language choice, a voice/text switch with automatic fallback when the mic is denied, a demo transcript and a sticky Analyze button.

Issues, ranked

High

Image assets don't exist, so emoji are the imagery

public/materials/ and public/brand/ are both missing. The landing hero is a 🏡 in a clay square. Zinc sheets render as 🏚️ (a derelict house), cement as 🪨 (a rock), and each OS draws these emoji differently.

Dark mode breaks every accent color

The dark theme redefines the soft tints but leaves --clay --brick --leaf --sky --amber unchanged. On the dark surface, brick text drops to about 2.0:1 contrast, sky to about 2.8:1 and clay to about 3.4:1. themeColor is also hard-coded to clay.

The most important warnings fail contrast in light mode

High

Each color has two or three meanings

Leaf is both the Heal & Sow category and “verified impact”, so a Heal & Sow card looks verified. Brick is both Reconstruction and errors or rejection. Clay is both the main button color and Roof & Shelter. Sky covers verified, in review, paused, received, delivered and used. Amber covers pledged, draft, demo and needs confirmation.

Med

Three component styles that contradict each other

The public pages use pill buttons, 2px borders and rounded-3xl. The field screens use rounded-xl and string constants for buttons. The coordinator uses rounded-md, ring-1 and a Button component. There are two CATEGORY_STYLE maps and three status-chip systems, and they disagree: paused is clay in the field screens but sky on the coordinator desk.

On /donate, “pledge ≠ delivery” appears four times: at the top, under the card, in the sheet and on the success screen. “Demo” shows up in five components (header banner, landing notice, DemoStamp, DemoTag on every card, DemoLabel). When amber is everywhere, people learn to skip it.

The deck sits in a 24rem side column while the title and filters get the wide column. Four buttons (Pass, Pledge, I have, I'll buy) all open the same sheet, which then asks the same question again. The drag stamp is sky blue, but pledges are amber everywhere else.

Med

The header mixes demo controls into the product chrome

Role switching is a <select> in the global header. The language toggle is a solid clay pill, the loudest element there and louder than Donate. Nav links have no active state. On mobile, the banner, header bar and scrolling nav take up about 120px of sticky space.

Fixes that apply whichever direction you pick

  1. Separate category from status. Give each program a shape and icon (Roof = triangle, Reconstruction = square, Heal & Sow = circle) and keep color for ledger stages only. Then green can mean “verified” and nothing else.
  2. Use one component kit with one Button, one Chip and one status map across the public, field and coordinator screens.
  3. State “pledge ≠ delivery” once per screen, where the decision is made (the quantity sheet), and let the ledger visual carry the message everywhere else.
  4. Move demo controls into a single “Modo demo” drawer. Make the language toggle a quiet ES | EN segmented control and add active states to the nav.
  5. Commission 12 material illustrations in one consistent style, plus three program images. This is the biggest perceived-quality gain for the effort.

Obra

The construction site as the interface. Site-sign yellow, concrete and rebar black, with condensed stencil type. Pledges appear as hazard tape, meaning not yet safe to count. The materials are the heroes, and every number reads at the scale of a site sign.

Tierra Viva

Recovery as something that grows. Flat Caribbean color blocks (sea teal, mango, hibiscus) on bright paper, with soft geometry and a plant-growth metaphor for the ledger: a pledge is a seed and verified impact is a flower. Warm and communal, and plays to Heal & Sow without leaving out the other two programs.

Type

  • Bricolage Grotesque 800 with tight tracking for headlines
  • The same family at 400 for body text, so there's one voice throughout

  • The community and local partners, and social sharing

Solves

  • Programs are identified by shape (roof triangle, brick square, seed circle), never by color
  • Feels hopeful rather than like a disaster site

Risk

  • A metaphor can blur exact meaning, so each glyph always needs a word next to it. The illustrations take more work to make

Señal Clara

A public utility you can read in direct sun. Navy, white and one signal blue, set in Atkinson Hyperlegible, a typeface built for low vision. Stages read like package tracking, written as full sentences. Dark mode is a designed theme in its own right, not an afterthought. This is the most accessible and least decorative direction.

Recibo

Every pledge gets a receipt. A pledge prints a ticket stub, and the nonprofit stamps it at each stage: carbon violet for the promise, blue for custody and a round green seal for verified. The follow-your-pledge page (/follow/[token]) becomes the hero of the brand. The monospace type comes from the thermal-receipt subject itself.

Type

  • Courier Prime for anything printed on a receipt
  • Familjen Grotesk for UI and stamps

Best for

  • Donor follow-up and weekly reports, where institutional donors want an audit trail

Solves

  • Shows pledge ≠ delivery without a warning: the stamp slots are visibly empty
  • A natural home for evidence: each stamp links to its receipt or photo

Risk

  • Feels transactional, closer to logistics than to people. Mono text in long passages is hard to read, so keep it on the tickets

Mosaico

“Cada ladrillo, contado,” taken literally. Every unit of material is one tile. A need for 200 bricks is a wall of 200 tiles, each colored by the furthest stage it has verifiably reached. You can't overstate impact when the empty tiles are right there. Restrained indigo type lets the wall carry the page.

  • Schibsted Grotesk 800 with tight tracking for headlines
  • Schibsted 400/600 for body and figures

  • The public dashboard, donors and the judging demo: the live wall filling in is the “aha”

  • Replaces six bars with one honest picture, without stacking the stages

  • Turns the brand line into the visual system

Risk

  • Large quantities (1,000+) need bundling, e.g. one tile = 10 units. The wall needs a table alternative for screen readers

Recommendation: Mosaico for public and donor screens, Señal Clara for working screens

Mosaico turns the headline you already have into the visual system, works without photography and makes the demo moment visible: 50 tiles go from hatched to blue to green live as the coordinator records receipt.

Mosaico's component rules (big targets, one status map, sentence-style stages, real dark mode) come from Señal Clara, which also styles the field and coordinator screens. Both use the same indigo, hatched amber and verified green, so the product still reads as one system.

Recibo's stamped stub is worth adopting just for /follow/[token] if you have time.
